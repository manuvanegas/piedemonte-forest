# Decisions: data

Next ID: A12.

- **A1** [SUPERSEDED → A5] **Roads: GRIP4 Region 2 rather than raw OSM.** For Colombia, GRIP4 blends OSM highways with IGAC photogrammetric mapping for lower road classes (Meijer et al. 2018). Informal roads remain undercounted, a limitation for any road dataset in this region.
- **A2** **CRS: EPSG:9377 (MAGNA-SIRGAS 2018 / Origen-Nacional) for all distances and areas.** Sources in EPSG:4326 are reprojected, or their pixel areas computed, before any metric calculation.
- **A3** **Raw inputs are read straight from their zip archives** (`zip://…!file.shp`). No extracted copies, and the archive's checksum identifies the version used.
- **A4** [SUPERSEDED → A9] **Rivers: IGAC 1:100.000 drainage (single- and double-line), queried from IDEAM's republication of the IGAC layers.** Digitised from imagery, covers rivers of all widths and carries names; IGAC's own server was unavailable (2026-10-23). The copy has no capture dates and no perennial/intermittent attribute. HydroRIVERS v1.0, modelled from SRTM elevation, is kept as a comparison.
- **A5** **Roads: GRIP4 as the pre-ceasefire road network.** Within the study area GRIP4 is 99.9% IGAC mapping dated 2012 (its source and year attributes), with no ferry or river-route class. Distance to road therefore means distance to the network as it stood before the ceasefire (D3). Roads opened later are not observed; clearing that follows them is captured by distance to earlier clearing, updated every year. Informal roads are undercounted.
- **A6** **Protected areas: current RUNAP boundaries for every year.** The RUNAP download holds only current boundaries, and its `fecha_insc` is the registry date (2011 or later for every area), not the designation date. The largest change in the study area is Chiribiquete: 1.30 Mha at creation (1989), enlarged to 2.78 Mha in 2013 (Resolución 1038) and 4.27 Mha in 2018 (Resolución 1256). Before 2018, distance to a protected area and the inside flag count parts of its extension as protected ahead of their designation.
- **A7** **Nearest-neighbour distances run against targets split into pieces of at most 256 vertices (`ST_Subdivide`).** The spatial index ranks candidates by bounding box, then measures the exact distance to each; large RUNAP polygons (up to 130,667 vertices; Chiribiquete's box covers 86,896 km²) make every candidate costly. On 10,000 sampled 2015 patches: 27.4 ms per patch on RUNAP as loaded (about 17 h for all years) vs 0.15 ms on the split copy, with the same nearest area and distance for every patch (`results/initial-checks/nearest_timing.csv`). The full run on the split copy, all 2,220,361 patches, took 5 min 20 s.
- **A8** **Distance targets are loaded within 200 km of the study area, and each run fails if any patch is 200 km or more from its nearest target.** A feature outside the loaded set can only be nearer than the one found when the distance found exceeds the buffer. The farthest patches are 129 km from a road and 105 km from a protected area, and 0.6% of patches are more than 50 km from a road, so a narrower buffer would miss the true nearest road for some of them. Roads are split like protected areas (A7), though they need it less: 0.32 vs 0.19 ms per patch on a 1,000-patch sample, with the same distances.
- **A9** **Rivers: IGAC double-line rivers only.** Single-line streams are everywhere: at 48,000 random 2007 forest points, distance to the nearest IGAC river runs from 0.05 to 0.75 km (10th to 90th percentile), narrower than one H3 resolution-7 hex (2.8 km corner to corner), so hexes would get nearly the same value. Double-line rivers, which IGAC maps as polygons rather than lines, run from 1.6 to 29.7 km, also more than 10 km from a road. HydroRIVERS discharge cutoffs of 5 and 20 m³/s spread similarly (1 m³/s sits at the hex limit), but each needs a threshold chosen in this project; IGAC's comes from its mapping (`results/exploration/covariate_spread.csv`). Narrower rivers used by small boats are missed.
- **A10** **Indigenous resguardos: ANT's formalized resguardo layer, at current boundaries for every year.** RUNAP lists only protected areas, while 240 resguardos cover 4.94 Mha (19%) of the study area, 0.74 Mha of it also protected. Distances to resguardos and to protected areas are kept separate; whether a patch lies in a protected area, a resguardo, both or neither follows from the two. The layer's only date is the last administrative act (constitution, enlargement or restructuring), 2015 or later for 101 of the 217 resguardos listed in the four departments, so boundaries can't be dated. ANT notes its maps are still being validated, and polygon areas differ from titled areas. 18 polygons nationwide have self-touching boundaries and are repaired with `make_valid` (no change in area).
- **A11** **Slope: SRTM 1 arc-second (2000), resampled bilinearly to the MapBiomas grid in Earth Engine; each patch gets the mean slope of its own pixels.** SRTM predates every loss year, is in the public domain and is the usual choice for slope in deforestation studies. It is a surface model: the radar partly reflects off the canopy, so slopes along the edges of clearings older than 2000 are overstated, and filled voids leave a few near-vertical artefacts (0.002% of study-area pixels at 80° or more). FABDEM, a bare-earth model, is built on Copernicus elevation from 2011–2015, the middle of the study period, and is licensed for non-commercial use only. Slope at 48,000 random 2007 forest points runs from 1.3° to 13.9° (10th to 90th percentile, median 3.7°; `results/exploration/slope_spread.csv`); loss patches have a median of 4.6°.
