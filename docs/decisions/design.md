# Decisions: design

Next ID: D18.

- **D1** **Study area: Caquetá, Guaviare, Meta, Putumayo (62 municipios, HDX COD-AB 2020).** The core of the Amazon deforestation frontier, spanning Andean foothills, Amazon lowland forest and Llanos savanna.
- **D2** [SUPERSEDED → D7] **Treatment: `FARC1` from the Prem et al. (2020) replication data** (at least one violent event attributed to the group, 2011–2014). It measures violent presence, not territorial control: a municipio held without contest can score 0. `FARC2` and `FARC_N` serve as robustness variants.
- **D3** **Timing: 2014 is the last pre-ceasefire year, 2015 the first post-ceasefire year**, following Prem et al.'s ceasefire indicator. Any baseline layer uses 2014 or earlier.
- **D4** **Forest loss from MapBiomas Colombia Collection 3, pinned.** Land-use classes (forest → pasture) rather than canopy change, one method across 1985–2024, and a history long enough to separate primary from secondary forest. Collections reprocess the full series, so the collection number is fixed.
- **D5** **Hansen GFC v1.13 is used only as a comparison, 2013 onward.** It records tree cover loss from any cause, and its detection sensitivity changes across 2011, 2013 and 2015 (Global Forest Watch technical notes), which would confound a before/after-2015 comparison.
- **D6** **Results are reported as associations.** Treatment is assigned to 62 municipios with no causal design. Pre-ceasefire loss enters as a control to show change, not effect.
- **D7** **Treatment: pre-ceasefire armed-group presence = `FARC1`, plus untreated municipios that share ≥ 80% of their border with treated ones and have at least one selective killing attributed to the group in 2011–2014 (`FARC2`, Centro Nacional de Memoria Histórica).** A municipio almost enclosed by violent presence, with killings recorded by a second source, is unlikely to be outside the group's reach. This adds four municipios (30 of 62 treated); any cutoff from 51% to 84% selects the same four. Prem et al. use `FARC1` alone. Like `FARC1`, it measures violent presence, not territorial control. `FARC_N` is not used: its construction is not unequivocally described in the paper.
- **D8** **Years: pre-period 2008–2014, post-period 2015–2023.** 2008 gives seven pre-ceasefire years to check parallel trends while staying after the 2004–2006 military offensives in these departments; 2011–2014 (the window of Prem et al.) serves as a sensitivity check. 2023 is the last year the loss rule (D9) can confirm, as it needs the following year's map.
- **D9** **Loss in year t: forest in t−2 and t−1, an anthropic class (D10) in t and t+1.** Requiring two years on each side keeps single-year classification flips out of the outcome; the rule follows the persistence criteria of MapBiomas Brazil's deforestation module. Clearings that regrow within a year are missed.
- **D10** **Anthropic classes: forest plantation (9), mosaic of agriculture and pasture (21), infrastructure (24), other non-vegetated area (25), mining (30), aquaculture (31), oil palm (35), banana (74), solar farm (75).** Natural forest replaced by plantation or palm counts as loss. Class 25 counts as anthropic: of the forest that became 25 in 2015, 96% was agriculture or pasture by 2018. Forest → water, wetland or natural grassland is not loss (in 2015, much of it reverted to forest within three years) and is reported separately.
- **D11** **Forest = forest (3) and flooded forest (6).** Colombia's national forest definition (IDEAM) sets structural thresholds and excludes plantations but not flooded forest, and MapBiomas Brazil's deforestation module counts flooded forest as natural vegetation. Seasonal flooding can cause spurious flips; MapBiomas Colombia removes flooded-forest pixels present in under 40% of years, and D10 counts only anthropic destinations.
- **D12** **Forest type at t−1: secondary if the pixel had two consecutive anthropic years since 1985 (the same test as D9), primary otherwise; `sec_age` = years since that clearing ended.** "Primary" means no recorded clearing since 1985. The record behind each loss year lengthens from 23 years (2008) to 38 (2023), so regrowth on old clearings only shows up as secondary in later years: 4% of 2023 loss was on land cleared more than 23 years earlier, which a 23-year record would count as primary. A fixed 23-year record for every year serves as a robustness check.
- **D13** **Loss patches: 4-connected groups of each year's loss pixels. Single-pixel patches are left out of every analysis (areas, rates, patch comparisons, emissions); comparisons with MapBiomas statistics and Hansen use all loss pixels.** Single pixels are more exposed to classification error than larger patches (mixed pixels at edges, small misalignments between years), and 65% of them are secondary forest, where young regrowth and pasture are easily confused. They make up 68% of patches and 19% of loss pixels. Colombia's official monitoring (IDEAM) removes changes under about 1 ha (9 px), following the minimum area in its forest definition (Galindo et al., 2014), so loss here is not directly comparable with official figures. Choosing the threshold properly would take checks such as repeat clearing of the same pixels, agreement with independent imagery by patch size, and whether single pixels sit in the same places as larger patches.
- **D14** **Distance to earlier clearing: to the nearest loss patch of the three previous years (t−3 to t−1), never the same year. Loss for 2005–2007 is computed with the same rule (D9, D13) only to serve as earlier clearings for 2008–2010; it enters no area, rate or emission.** Measured against all patches since the first loss year, the target set grows every year and distances shrink for that reason alone: on 2,000 sampled patches per year, the 90th percentile fell from 803 m (2009) to 150 m (2021) and the share of patches touching an earlier one rose from 28% to 68%, while with a three-year window, over all patches, the 90th percentile stays between 379 and 481 m and the share touching between 35% and 44% in every year from 2008 to 2023. A one-year window responds more to a clearing detected a year early or late; with five years, the median is already 0 m in 2015. About 40% of patches touch an earlier one (distance 0), so the share at 0 m is reported with the median.
- **D15** **Annual loss rate: loss in year t divided by remaining forest, the area that was forest in both t−2 and t−1, summed over each group's municipios.** Only those pixels can be flagged as loss in t (D9), so the rate counts loss and forest on the same footing, and forest that regrows and is cleared again enters both. A fixed 2007 baseline would understate later years as forest shrinks: by 2023, remaining forest is 5.5% smaller than 2007 forest in municipios without pre-ceasefire armed-group presence and 6.7% smaller in those with it.
- **D16** **Forest types are named old-growth and secondary: old-growth = forest with no recorded clearing since 1985 under D12's test (primary forest plus regrowth already standing in 1985); secondary = regrowth on land cleared since 1985.** The record cannot tell primary forest from regrowth older than 1985, so "primary", which claims no past clearing, says more than the data show; "old-growth" is defined here by the record, not by forest structure. D12's rule is unchanged; its "primary" reads as old-growth.
- **D17** **Patch-size classes, by patch area: small under 5 ha, medium 5–25 ha, large over 25 ha.**
