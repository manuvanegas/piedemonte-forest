# Where the loss happened

Supplementary result to the [README](../../README.md#where-the-loss-happened). The patch attributes are described under [Landscape layers](../../README.md#landscape-layers), and the two municipio groups in [Pre-ceasefire armed-group presence](../../README.md#pre-ceasefire-armed-group-presence). Like the main results, this describes patterns in the data: it reports no statistical tests and makes no causal claims.

![Six panels by year and pre-ceasefire armed-group presence: median distance of loss patches to the closest road and river, share of patches touching an earlier clearing, median patch slope, and share of loss area inside or touching a protected area or an Indigenous resguardo](../../figures/loss/access_by_year.png)

**Figure 1.** Where the loss happened, by year and pre-ceasefire armed-group presence.

Distance to roads and rivers, contact with earlier clearings and slope changed little in either group. Two shares rose: loss inside or touching a protected area in Presence Municipios, and loss inside or touching a resguardo in No-Presence Municipios.

An earlier clearing is a loss patch of the three previous years. Full table, with shares as fractions: [`results/loss/before_after.csv`](../../results/loss/before_after.csv).

Loss in Presence Municipios lies about twice as far from wide rivers as in No-Presence Municipios, before and after the ceasefire (medians of 7.5 and 8.4 km, against 3.5 and 3.8 km), in line with their territory ([README, Figure 2](../../README.md#landscape-layers)). Its distance to roads differs much less (1.3 against 1.0 km before the ceasefire), even though Presence Municipios have less than half the road length per km²: in both groups, clearing stays close to roads, while half of the 2007 forest in the study area lay more than 14 km from a mapped road. After the ceasefire the median patch lies about 0.3 km further from the closest road in both groups; the road layer dates from 2012, so part of that shift may be clearing along roads it does not show. The share of patches touching an earlier clearing and the median slope barely moved.

The year-by-year shares show more than the period averages in Table 1. In Presence Municipios, loss inside or touching a protected area made up 5–8% of loss area a year before the ceasefire and 9–12% in 2018–2022. In No-Presence Municipios, loss inside or touching a resguardo rose from 10–13% to 13–18% in 2017–2022, peaking in 2020. Both layers apply today's boundaries to every year. For protected areas, this counts parts of Chiribiquete as protected before its 2013 and 2018 enlargements, which raises the earlier shares, so the rise is understated. The same likely holds for resguardos: 101 of the 217 in the four departments had their last boundary change in 2015 or later ([A6](../decisions/data.md), [A10](../decisions/data.md)).
